# Viva Guide — Deliverable 3

One-page evidence map, likely questions, and defensible answers. Every number
below is regenerated by the commands in the README and stored in the committed
`results/` and `report/generated/` files.

## 1. Thirty-Second Pitch

We asked whether a learned heuristic that predicts exact cost-to-goal can reduce
A\* node expansions relative to Manhattan. The model fits the target well
($R^2=0.860$), but the hypothesis fails end-to-end: raw neural A\* expands 557
nodes versus 181 for Manhattan (+208%, $p<10^{-46}$), and Manhattan clipping
restores parity without a gain (188 nodes, +4.0%). The result is not a training
failure; it is a structural failure of the learned field. A paired analysis
shows adjacent-cell inconsistency, systematic distance bias, and a concentration
of harm on easy mazes. We report this as a measured negative result and expose
the mechanism with a component ablation.

## 4. Likely Questions

**Why does a high $R^2$ coexist with worse search?**
$R^2$ is an average cell-level fit. A\* is sensitive to ordering and to
consistency: small errors near the goal change which frontier nodes look
promising. The raw field overestimates near the goal and underestimates far
away, so it distorts the ordering even when the average error is small. The
consistency table makes this explicit.

**Why does clipping not help?**
Clipping guarantees admissibility but not informativeness. When the prediction
exceeds Manhattan, clipping collapses it to Manhattan; when it is below, it
preserves an underestimate. The resulting field is close to Manhattan with
extra prediction cost, hence 188 vs 181 nodes and 2.68 vs 1.89 ms. It is a safety
mechanism, not an efficiency mechanism.

**Is the comparison fair?**
All heuristics use the same A\* implementation, tie-breaking, maze set, and
success criterion. The neural field is computed once per maze and cached, so
runtime includes feature construction and forward inference. The oracle is a
diagnostic lower bound, not a deployable baseline.

**Why use a scikit-learn fallback?**
The committed PyTorch checkpoint is a one-epoch smoke test. Reporting its
numbers as a trained result would be misleading. The fallback uses the same
29-feature interface and target normalization and is small enough to commit, so
the benchmark is reproducible on CPU. The torch path is audited separately.

**What does the ablation prove?**
Goal offsets carry almost all predictive signal: removing them drops $R^2$ from
0.865 to 0.068 and inflates expansions to 1115. Coordinates alone or wall
window alone collapse. The wall window adds regression accuracy but worsens
expansions (268 vs 229), so better fit is not better search.

**Why is the failure worse on easy mazes?**
Low-density and direct mazes are where Manhattan is already near-perfect. The
learned field introduces ordering errors without offering a large potential
saving. On severe tortuosity the ratio is 1.51×, still worse than Manhattan but
less catastrophic than the 5.80× on direct mazes.

**What is adjacent-pair inconsistency?**
For neighboring free cells, $|h(u)-h(v)|$ should be at most 1 because a move
changes the true cost by at most 1. The raw field violates this on 64.1% of
pairs, which is a direct signature of a non-consistent heuristic and explains
re-expansions.

**Are the p-values corrected for multiple comparisons?**
The paired Wilcoxon signed-rank tests are reported per comparison and should be
read as exploratory; the confidence intervals come from a percentile bootstrap.
The effect sizes and intervals are the primary evidence, and the p-values are far
below any reasonable threshold.

**What would you change next?**
Train a consistency-aware objective (pairwise Lipschitz penalty), calibrate
near the goal, and evaluate a learned tie-breaking rule rather than replacing
the heuristic. A multi-seed trained PyTorch model is the first missing artefact.

**What are the main threats to validity?**
Synthetic static mazes, one generator pair, one headline seed, a 24-maze search
subset for the ablation, and a smoke-test torch checkpoint. The report states
each of these and avoids generalizing beyond the corpus.

**How do you reproduce the headline result?**
`python scripts/run_evaluation.py --backend sklearn --split test --output results/evaluation`,
then `python scripts/make_phase5_report_assets.py`. The fallback joblib files and
their SHA-256 hashes are committed in `models/README.md`.

## 5. Five-Minute Demo Script

1. `python -m pytest -q` — show 170 passed, 2 skipped.
2. `python scripts/demo.py --case good` — neural wins slightly.
3. `python scripts/demo.py --case bad` — 988 vs 57 expansions; point to the raw
   field and the over/under-estimation pattern.
4. `python scripts/demo.py --case typical --mode clipped_manhattan` — parity but
   no gain.
5. Open `report/main.pdf` at the Phase 5 tables and the sample traces.
6. If asked for the torch path: `python scripts/run_torch_search.py` — show the
   audit refusing to benchmark the smoke checkpoint.
